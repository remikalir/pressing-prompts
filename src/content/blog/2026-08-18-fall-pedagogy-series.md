*Pressing Prompts* is grounded in principles of critical pedagogy and educational experimentation. Unlike a static set of so-called best practices, we encourage instructors and students to examine how AI raises crucial questions about our academic responsibilities, knowledge, labor, judgment, and learning.

With the new academic year starting soon, we're inviting our colleagues at Duke to learn more about the project as they thoughtfully integrate AI ethics into their classrooms.

This fall semester, the [Duke Center for Teaching and Learning](https://ctl.duke.edu/) and [Duke University Libraries](https://library.duke.edu/) will host a *Pressing Prompts* pedagogy series. This series will include both workshops and conversations. We're excited to convene a community of instructors who are asking urgent questions about the ethical dimensions of AI. The series will offer faculty guidance about teaching with *Pressing Prompts*, space to reflect on their pedagogical experimentation with the resource, and opportunities to iterate *Pressing Prompts* based on their use cases and experiences.

Duke instructors curious about critical approaches to AI and education are welcome to join us for one, some, or all of the following opportunities.

* Workshop (on campus): Thursday, September 10, 10:00-11:00 AM, Rubenstein 249.
* Workshop (online): Friday, September 11, 12:00-1:00 PM, Zoom.
* Community conversations on: Friday, October 9; Friday, November 13; and Friday, December 11; all from 12:00-1:00 PM via Zoom.

In both workshops, we will: present *Pressing Prompts* within the larger context of AI, ethics, and education; scaffold opportunities to experiment with *Pressing Prompts* as we help instructors consider how it might fit into classroom conversations, assignments, or larger course units; and facilitate conversation for sharing ideas between faculty peers.

And across the three community conversations, we will: spend time hearing from faculty who have experimented with *Pressing Prompts* in their classrooms; answer questions and brainstorm together about how instructors might integrate the resource into their pedagogy and courses; and learn from faculty about their interest in and ideas for iterating *Pressing Prompts* for future use.

Finally, a note for our many dear colleagues not at Duke: We'd love to organize similar activities for you, too\! This past summer, we facilitated workshops about *Pressing Prompts* for various groups, including [local K-12 educators](https://pressingprompts.org/blog/we-the-people-workshop) and instructors around the world during [MYFest26](https://youtu.be/Vlv7tLU2sKA?si=iMOR_DkCIO5bUiTo). Please contact us to discuss how we can share *Pressing Prompts* with you and your colleagues-whether on campus or online, and whether through workshops, conversions, or other types of professional learning opportunities.
